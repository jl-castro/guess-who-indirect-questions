# Product

## Register

product

## Users

English teachers standing at a projector in a brightly lit classroom. Students sit at desks, look at the big screen, and speak out loud. The teacher operates the board; the class never touches the UI. Success is that the back row can read names, see who is still in play, and feel they are playing a game together.

## Product Purpose

A teacher-controlled Guess Who board for practicing English indirect questions. The software does not ask or judge language. It keeps the secret character, counts valid questions, lets the teacher eliminate faces, and hosts the final guess. The classroom conversation is the product; the screen is the shared game piece.

## Brand Personality

Warm, toy-like, competitive. Like the physical Guess Who board brought into class: plastic tiles, a cream frame, a little theatrical reveal. Voice is short and game-like, never instructional waffle.

## Anti-references

- SaaS admin dashboards: dark navy panels, identical cards, sidebar control stacks, hero metrics.
- Kindergarten / kawaii education apps: balloon letters, too-cute mascots, primary-color chaos aimed at small children.
- Generic "AI dark tool" chrome and glass cards.

## Design Principles

- The board is the game. Teacher controls serve the board; they never compete with it.
- Play at projector scale. Names, states, and remaining options must read from the back of the room.
- Feel like a physical toy, not a settings panel.
- Language practice stays spoken. On-screen copy is short, present-tense, and never lectures.
- One glance tells you: secret locked or not, questions left, who is still in.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Priority is projection: high contrast, large type, unmistakable eliminated vs in-play states. Do not rely on color alone for eliminated cards. Reduced motion should skip tile flips. Keyboard is secondary to click/tap at the lectern.
